Privacy policy

Effective 16 November 2026. This policy covers the Spot On app for iPhone, including the update that adds ads and optional purchases. Earlier versions showed no ads and offered no purchases; for them, the sections on ads and purchases don't apply.

The short version: Spot On has no accounts and we don't collect your name, email or scores: what the game remembers stays on your iPhone. The game shows ads through Google AdMob. With your permission those ads can be personalised, which means Google and its partners use your device's advertising identifier and related information. You can say no, and you can remove ads with a one-time purchase. Purchases are handled by Apple.

What we collect

We, the makers of Spot On, collect nothing ourselves. Spot On doesn't ask for your name, email, contacts or photos, and it has no sign-in. We run no servers that receive your data, and your scores, stats and settings are never sent to us.

Two other companies' software runs inside the app and does handle information: Google (for ads) and Apple (for purchases). The sections below say what each does.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Spot On shows ads between some runs, and offers an optional ad you can choose to watch for an extra life. Ads are served by Google AdMob using the Google Mobile Ads SDK. No ad is shown, and no ad is requested, during your first session or before you finish or skip the tutorial.

To serve and measure ads, Google and its advertising partners may collect and use:

  • your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow tracking (see below), and other device identifiers;
  • your approximate location, worked out from your IP address;
  • your interactions with ads (which were shown, tapped or watched) and with the app as it relates to ads;
  • diagnostic information: crash data, performance data and other technical data about the app and your device.

Google uses this for advertising (including personalised advertising where you've allowed it), for measuring ads, and for analytics and fraud prevention. Google acts under its own terms: see How Google uses information from apps that use its services and the Google privacy policy. We don't receive this information about you; we see only totals, such as how many ads were shown.

Your choices about ads

  • The tracking prompt. After the tutorial, iOS asks whether to allow Spot On to track your activity across other companies' apps and websites. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, the advertising identifier isn't shared and ads are not personalised with it. You can change this any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • The consent form (EEA, UK and Switzerland). If you're in one of these regions, a Google consent form appears before any ad is requested. You can consent, refuse, or manage your choices by purpose and by partner, and you can reopen the form at any time from Settings → Privacy choices in the game.
  • Remove ads. A one-time purchase in Settings removes the ads between runs for good and makes the extra life free, with no ad. Once it's owned the game no longer loads Google's ad software for ads, and asks for no consent or tracking permission.
  • iOS also lets you limit ad personalisation by Apple and reset permissions in Settings.

Purchases (Apple App Store)

Spot On offers two optional one-time purchases: Practice mode and Remove ads. Payment is handled entirely by Apple through your Apple Account; we never see your payment details, name or email. The app asks the App Store which of these purchases your Apple Account owns, so it can unlock them and so that Restore purchases works on a new device. That record of what you own is kept on your iPhone. Apple's handling of purchases is covered by the Apple privacy policy.

The game engine

Spot On is made with the Unity game engine. Unity may send Unity Technologies limited anonymous technical information about your device and how well the engine runs on it. This doesn't include your name, your scores or anything you type, and we don't receive it. Purchases use Unity's in-app purchasing code, which talks to the App Store on your device and sends nothing about your purchases to Unity. Unity's handling of engine information is covered by the Unity privacy policy.

What stays on your iPhone

To work as a game, Spot On saves a few things in its own storage on your device:

  • your best score;
  • your stats for each challenge (rounds played, pass rate, recent accuracy, best streaks) and the highest level of each you've reached for practice;
  • your daily challenge results and your daily streak;
  • your settings (sound, haptics, beat haptics, left-handed, colour-blind, tap-timing calibration) and which tutorials, new challenges and rule twists you've already seen;
  • which purchases you own, how many times you've opened the game, and when you were last asked to rate it (so you aren't asked often).

This information isn't sent to us or to the ad provider. It may be included in your iPhone's own backups (iCloud or computer), which Apple's and your own settings control. Deleting the app deletes it.

Sharing

When you tap Share or Challenge, Spot On opens the standard iOS share sheet with a short text, such as your score, level and a challenge code. Nothing is sent unless you pick where it goes, and then it's handled by the app you chose (for example Messages), under that app's privacy terms. A challenge code holds only the run's random seed, the game version, your score and the level reached.

Opening a Spot On challenge link (spoton://) starts that run on your device; the link isn't sent anywhere.

Rating the game and contacting support

After a good run the game may ask whether you're enjoying it. Your answer isn't sent anywhere. A high rating opens Apple's own rating prompt; otherwise you're offered a button that opens an email to us in your mail app, which sends only what you choose to write.

Tracking

"Tracking" here means linking information about you or your device from Spot On with information from other companies' apps and websites for advertising. It happens only through Google's ad software and only if you allow it in the iOS prompt. We don't sell your information.

Children

Spot On isn't directed at children under 13 (or the age your country sets), and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has allowed tracking or consented to personalised ads on a device, you can turn tracking off in iOS Settings and reopen the consent choices in the game, or contact us.

Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have the right to see, correct or delete personal information, or to object to its use. We hold no personal information about you ourselves. For information Google holds from ads, use the choices above and Google's My Ad Center; for purchase records, your Apple Account. You can also write to us and we'll help where we can.

Changes

If Spot On ever changes how it handles information, this page will be updated before the change ships, with a new effective date.
